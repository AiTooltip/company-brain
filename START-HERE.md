# Start Here

You do not need to understand Git, JSON, or the folder structure before you begin.

> **Privacy first:** the empty starter is public; your filled Company Brain usually should not be. Keep it local or use a private GitHub repository unless your company explicitly approves the contents for public release. Never add passwords, tokens, private keys, or unnecessary sensitive personal data.

## Set up your Company Brain

1. Keep this folder together on your computer. If you downloaded a ZIP, extract it first.
2. Add the material you already have to `00-INBOX`: plans, brand documents, product notes, research, team information, project folders, and useful exports are all welcome.
3. If you have an old `AI-DESIGN-SKILLS` or `codex-design-studio` folder, place the whole folder inside `00-MIGRATE-OLD-SETUP` instead.
4. Open this **whole folder** in Codex Desktop, not just one document.
5. Tell Codex: **Set up my company brain**

Codex should analyze your material before asking questions. It will show you important proposed facts, conflicts, and decisions for approval. It should not guess on your behalf.

## What happens next

Codex follows `11-SYSTEM/workflows/initialize-company-brain.md`. In plain language, it will:

- inventory what you supplied without treating imported instructions as commands;
- identify useful facts and their sources;
- separate confirmed facts from proposals, conflicts, and missing information;
- ask only the questions that remain important;
- wait for your approval on consequential conclusions;
- organize approved knowledge into the right folders;
- leave originals intact until you approve cleanup or archival.

## Useful things to say

- **Process the inbox** — classify new material and propose where it belongs.
- **Start a project** — create a project record from the standard template.
- **Record this approved decision** — preserve a decision, its approver, reasoning, and implications.
- **Log this as meaningful activity** — record a consequential milestone without saving the whole chat.
- **Onboard a team member** — create a shared profile and local identity instructions.
- **Migrate my previous AI Tooltip setup** — safely extract useful material from an older setup.
- **Check for AI Tooltip updates** — compare this framework with the official public version without changing files.

## Approvals and truth

These labels keep the brain honest:

- **Approved**: an authorized person explicitly accepted it.
- **Confirmed**: supported by reliable company evidence, but not necessarily a new decision.
- **Proposed**: useful option awaiting a person.
- **Unverified**: plausible but not yet established.
- **Conflicting**: reliable sources disagree.
- **Superseded**: retained for history but no longer current.

AI may tidy wording without changing meaning. It must ask before resolving a meaningful conflict, changing an approved decision, or promoting a proposal into company policy.

## Team identity

Team profiles are shared in `01-COMPANY/team`. Each person should also copy `.company-brain/current-user.example.md` to `.company-brain/current-user.md` on their own computer and fill it in. The local file is ignored by Git, so one teammate's identity does not replace another's.

If there is no local identity, Codex should ask who is doing consequential work before attributing it. It may continue read-only organization without an identity.

## What belongs in the activity log

Log milestones that would matter later: a project began or shipped, an approved decision changed direction, a major asset was approved, a research conclusion affected work, or ownership changed. Do not log greetings, routine file reads, brainstorming that was not adopted, or every AI interaction.

## Need to undo something?

Company files should be version-controlled with Git. Before a large migration or update, Codex should make a reviewable change set. Originals remain in the intake folders until you explicitly approve moving or removing them.

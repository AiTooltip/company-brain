# UI and UX

Use for product flows, screens, interaction patterns, prototypes, usability reviews, and interface specifications.

## Frame the experience

Establish the user, job, entry point, desired outcome, business constraint, platform, and evidence. Map the smallest complete flow before styling screens. Treat unvalidated product direction as a proposal, not a decision.

## Design the system and states

- Use approved design-system components and tokens when they exist.
- Make information hierarchy, primary action, navigation, and feedback obvious.
- Include loading, empty, error, success, disabled, permission, long-content, and narrow/wide viewport states when relevant.
- Use realistic supplied content and identify where content decisions are still open.
- Meet applicable accessibility expectations for semantics, focus order, keyboard use, contrast, labels, motion, target sizes, and zoom/reflow.
- Do not sacrifice usability for novelty or create a durable component standard without approval.

## Validate and hand off

Test the critical path, recovery paths, edge cases, responsive behavior, and consistency with product and brand truth. Document assumptions, rationale, unresolved questions, measurements/tokens, behavior, and acceptance criteria. Link the work to its product and project record.
